# Lead-level skills

For the lead repo: the CEO (strategy, vision, Go/No-Go, idea intake) and the Product Visionary (market, competition, go-to-market, product-market fit). Lead work is text and judgement no test settles, and no model is trained on how a DUDES company decides (`CORE.md` §5, *Where skills earn their place*) — so the skills here carry that method. They share this background instead of repeating it:

- **A decision is a commit.** It carries its role tag (`[🔹CEO]`), lands in the lead repo's documents and states its premise and what would falsify it. The decision log is derived from those commits (`make audit`), never kept by hand.
- **The record comes first.** Before a judgement, collect what the mechanisms already know — the decision log, each product's derived item status (`dudes item board`, F70), the consistency check, the roadmap's milestones. Without the engine, read the same facts from the backlog, the QRs and git; `dudes` only derives them. A judgement without that record is an opinion.
- **Sources are primary and dated, and evidence has a level.** A market claim names its source, its date and whether it is documented or measured; a forecast names what would falsify it. Each lead document declares one level in its header: *hypothesis* (reasoning only), *messaging-level* (public market messaging, secondary research, a competitor's own claims) or *field-validated* (primary data: interviews, pilots, telemetry, with source and sample). A level rises only with the data that earns it, never silently. **Evidence before edit:** below field-validated, a lead document changes only for new primary data (the commit names the source), a recorded decision, the correction of a wrong statement, or a change that ripples down from a higher document. More desk research waits for the data. The instance's lead gate goal can check that every document declares a level; whether it rose honestly is the CEO's judgement.
- **One document, one question.** Each document type answers one question, names what it must not contain and hands a defined thing to the next. A question answered in the wrong document is a defect: move the answer, do not copy it. On a conflict the higher document in the chain wins and the lower one is fixed. The spec level shares this contract (`skills/spec/README.md`); the instance's own document types and their rows live in its repos. **An exercise** (a press release written ahead, its Q&A, a workshop task) sits beside the chain: it yields signals that land in the owner documents through normal commits, owns no ID, is never cited as evidence, and may be deleted afterwards. Its file stays outside the spec-set's roots, so no check reads an ID from it.
- **The company's own** — its lab, infrastructure, company document, local conventions — lives in the instance's lead repo: what can be checked as a goal of its makefile, what needs judgement as its own `skills/`. A company skill that extends one here names it instead of copying it.

**Company audit — no skill.** The record is programmatic: `make audit ARGS='--since=<last audit>'` (the decisions), `dudes item board` and `dudes item check` per product (derived status, consistency), `dudes status` (runs waiting for a decision), and the instance lead repo's own goals for what only the company knows (its lab, its infrastructure). Without the engine, read the same facts from the backlog (item status), the QRs (verdict and verified commit per release) and `git log --grep='🔹'` (the decisions and the runs, by role). The judgement on that record is the CEO's: which premise of a past decision no longer holds, what work serves no goal and which goal has no work, what passed its review date untouched, and which decision unblocks a late milestone. A finding that needs a decision becomes one — a role-tagged commit.

Which role loads which skill in the role-play: `rules/DUDES.md` §1.
