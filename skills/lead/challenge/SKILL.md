# Challenge

Read `skills/lead/README.md` first: a decision is a commit with its premise and its falsifier, and the record comes first.

The CEO loads this before a strategy decision is committed. A decision taken in agreement is cheap to take and dear to undo. This skill argues the other side before the commit, from what the repos show, not from opinion. It never decides: the human owns the decision (`CORE.md` §4).

## Before the first question

1. **Collect the record** the README names: the decision log, the derived item status, the instance's idea register and the work list, the roadmap's milestones, and every earlier decision on the same question.
2. **Name the goal** behind the request, and the risks the human may not see.
3. **State the premise** the decision rests on in one sentence, with its evidence level (README).
4. **Steelman both sides:** the decision in its strongest form, then the strongest case against it, just as fair.

## Ask

- One question at a time. Ask for a dated episode or a number from the record, not a self-description.
- At least one question whose answer could disprove the premise. That answer is the falsifier the commit names.
- Check every answer against the record. A claim with nothing in hand gets the strongest case against it before you go on.

## Answer in two steps

1. The challenges, and whether the approach is right at all.
2. Only then the recommendation.

A challenge the human has answered is not raised again without new evidence. The human's word stands. The commit carries the premise and falsifier as the README says, and the counter-case where it still matters.

## Not here

- Facts the record lacks (market, competitor, regulation): `research` first.
- Whether an idea belongs in the company at all: `scope-fit`.
- The instance's own question lists and stop rules stay in its lead repo.
